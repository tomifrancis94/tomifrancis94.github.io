9 Are the axioms requirements of rationality?

  • Completeness. Must every pair be comparable?
  • Continuity. Is nothing infinitely better than anything else?
  • Transitivity. Next week.
  • Independence. Now: the Allais paradox.
9.1 The Allais paradox#
  • Option 1: \$1{,}000{,}000 for certain. Option 2: \$5{,}000{,}000 with probability 0.10, \$1{,}000{,}000 with 0.89, \$0 with 0.01.
  • Then: Option 3: \$1{,}000{,}000 with 0.11, else \$0. Option 4: \$5{,}000{,}000 with 0.10, else \$0.
  • Most people: Option 1 over 2, and Option 4 over 3. Allais, 1953. Savage himself answered that way at first. Thinking it over, he became convinced that the pattern was irrational, and changed his preferences.
Write Z for: \$5{,}000{,}000 with probability \nicefrac{10}{11}, else \$0.

  • Within each pair the second column is shared. Writing M for \$1{,}000{,}000 and O for \$0: Option 1 = \mix{M}{0.11}{M}, Option 2 = \mix{Z}{0.11}{M}, Option 3 = \mix{M}{0.11}{O}, Option 4 = \mix{Z}{0.11}{O}.
  • Independence: Option 1 \succsim Option 2 iff M \succsim Z iff Option 3 \succsim Option 4. So Option 1 \succ Option 2 together with Option 4 \succ Option 3 violates Independence.
9.2 Ellsberg’s urn#
30 red balls, and 60 that are black or yellow in an unknown proportion. One ball is drawn.
red (30)black (?)yellow (?)
Bet I\$100\$0\$0
Bet II\$0\$100\$0
Bet III\$100\$0\$100
Bet IV\$0\$100\$100
  • Most people: I \succ II, and IV \succ III.
  • Within each pair the yellow column is shared. The sure-thing principle says I \succsim II iff III \succsim IV.
  • No probabilistic credence in “black” makes both preferences expected-utility maximising.
9.3 Responses#
  • Bite the bullet: the pattern is a mistake, and on reflection people revise it.
  • Redescribe the outcomes: \$0 after turning down a sure million is not the same outcome as \$0. Then Independence isn’t violated, but it becomes hard to violate anything.
  • Give up Independence: theories that weight probabilities by rank, or by the agent’s attitude to risk.
