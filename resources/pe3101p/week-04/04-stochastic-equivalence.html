    1 / 2
    Principle (Stochastic equivalence)

    If two gambles give every final outcome the same probability, then the agent is indifferent between them.

    Only the chances of the final outcomes matter, not which states carry them. Stochastic equivalence is what lets us talk about the chances of outcomes instead of the states.

    It can be doubted. The doubts mostly arise when preferences have gaps — when Completeness fails. See Hare, “Take the Sugar”, Analysis 70 (2010); Francis and Gustafsson, “Stochastic Dominance for Incomplete Preferences”, Analysis 86 (2026), 299–311.
