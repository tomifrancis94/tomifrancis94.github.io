6 The remaining axioms

6.1 Completeness#
Principle (Completeness)

For all prospects A and B: A \succsim B or B \succsim A.

No gaps: every pair is comparable. With transitivity, everything lines up.

6.2 Independence#
Principle (Independence)

For all prospects A, B, C and every p \in (0,1): A \succsim B iff \mix{A}{p}{C} \succsim \mix{B}{p}{C} iff \mix{C}{p}{A} \succsim \mix{C}{p}{B}.

One way to realise these prospects is with gambles that agree if \neg E. But any gambles with these prospects will do; they need not agree anywhere. The principle is a biconditional, in weak preference, and both directions matter. Apply it twice: \mix{A}{p}{C} \succsim \mix{B}{p}{C} iff A \succsim B iff \mix{A}{q}{C} \succsim \mix{B}{q}{C}. So the weight makes no difference.

Principle (Sure-thing principle)

Gambles G, G', H, H' and an event E. If G, H agree if E and so do G', H', while G, G' agree if \neg E and so do H, H': then G \succsim G' iff H \succsim H'.

Take two gambles that agree on what happens if \neg E, and replace the shared outcome C by another shared outcome D. The sure-thing principle says that the two comparisons come out the same way. It is a principle about states: what the two gambles share cannot affect the comparison. Independence implies it, and with stochastic equivalence the two come to the same thing, at least when there are finitely many states.

6.3 Continuity#
Principle (Continuity)

If A \succ B \succ C, then there is some p \in (0,1) with B \sim \mix{A}{p}{C}.

1 / 4

So no prospect is infinitely better than another. When Continuity fails: an agent ranks b \succ c \succ a but prefers c to every mixture of b and a, however heavily weighted towards b. Then there is no p, and no number to assign to c. The question is whether such preferences could be rational.

6.4 The axioms#
TransitivityA \succsim B and B \succsim C give A \succsim C
CompletenessA \succsim B or B \succsim A
IndependenceA \succsim B iff \mix{A}{p}{C} \succsim \mix{B}{p}{C} iff \mix{C}{p}{A} \succsim \mix{C}{p}{B}
ContinuityA \succ B \succ C gives some p with B \sim \mix{A}{p}{C}

Reflexivity follows from Completeness (take B to be A), so it is dropped. Four axioms. Stochastic equivalence sits behind all of them: it is why \succsim ranges over prospects at all.
