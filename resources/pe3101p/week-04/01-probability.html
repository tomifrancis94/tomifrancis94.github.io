1 Three things about probability

1.1 Conditional credences are credences#
Proposition 1 (conditional credences are credences)

Suppose \Cr satisfies the probability axioms and \Cr(B) > 0. Define \Cr_B(A) = \Cr(A \mid B). Then \Cr_B satisfies the probability axioms.

So everything we know about credences carries over after conditioning. There are three axioms, and so three things to check.

Proof of Proposition 1 (conditional credences are credences)

Non-negativity. \Cr(A \mid B) = \Cr(A \cap B) / \Cr(B): a non-negative number divided by a positive one.

Normalisation. \Cr(\Omega \mid B) = \Cr(\Omega \cap B)/\Cr(B) = \Cr(B)/\Cr(B) = 1.

Finite additivity. Take X \cap Y = \varnothing. We want \Cr(X \cup Y \mid B) = \Cr(X \mid B) + \Cr(Y \mid B). Starting from the left,

\begin{aligned} \Cr(X \cup Y \mid B) &= \frac{\Cr((X \cup Y) \cap B)}{\Cr(B)} && \text{definition} \\ &= \frac{\Cr((X \cap B) \cup (Y \cap B))}{\Cr(B)} && \text{intersection distributes over union} \\ &= \frac{\Cr(X \cap B) + \Cr(Y \cap B)}{\Cr(B)} && \text{finite additivity: the two pieces are disjoint} \\ &= \Cr(X \mid B) + \Cr(Y \mid B). && \text{definition, twice} \end{aligned}
∎
1.2 Expectation#

A random variable X is a quantity whose value is settled by which outcome occurs. It takes one of the values x_1, \dots, x_n. Write X = x_j for the event that it takes the value x_j.

Definition (Expectation)

Where x_1, \dots, x_n are the possible values of X,

\E[X] = \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_j \cdot \Cr(X = x_j).

For a die, \E[X] = 1 \cdot \nicefrac{1}{6} + \dots + 6 \cdot \nicefrac{1}{6} = 3.5. Expected utility is the expectation of the random variable “utility of the outcome”.

Definition (Conditional expectation)

Where x_1, \dots, x_n are the possible values of X and \Cr(B) > 0,

\E[X \mid B] = \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_j \cdot \Cr(X = x_j \mid B).

This is the same formula, run with the conditioned credences. Those are credences, by Proposition 1 (conditional credences are credences), so this is a genuine expectation: the expectation of X under \Cr_B.

1.3 The law of total expectation#
Proposition 2 (law of total expectation)

Where B_1, \dots, B_m partition \Omega, each with \Cr(B_i) > 0,

\E[X] = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \Cr(B_i)\,\E[X \mid B_i].

The expectation is the credence-weighted average of the conditional expectations. Compare total probability: \Cr(A) = \sum_i \Cr(B_i)\,\Cr(A \mid B_i).

For example, roll a die and let X be the number shown. Split \Omega into odd and even. Then \E[X \mid \text{odd}] = \nicefrac{1}{3}(1 + 3 + 5) = 3, and \E[X \mid \text{even}] = \nicefrac{1}{3}(2 + 4 + 6) = 4. So \E[X] = \Cr(\text{odd}) \cdot 3 + \Cr(\text{even}) \cdot 4 = \nicefrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 + \nicefrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 = 3.5.

Proof of Proposition 2 (law of total expectation)

Start from the right-hand side.

\begin{aligned} \sum_{i} \Cr(B_i)\,\E[X \mid B_i] &= \sum_{i} \Cr(B_i) \sum_{j} x_j\,\Cr(X = x_j \mid B_i) && \text{definition of } \E[X \mid B_i] \\ &= \sum_{i} \sum_{j} x_j\,\Cr(B_i)\,\Cr(X = x_j \mid B_i) && \text{move } \Cr(B_i) \text{ inside} \\ &= \sum_{i} \sum_{j} x_j\,\Cr(\{X = x_j\} \cap B_i) && \text{definition of conditional probability} \\ &= \sum_{j} x_j \sum_{i} \Cr(\{X = x_j\} \cap B_i) && \text{swap the sums} \\ &= \sum_{j} x_j\,\Cr(X = x_j) && \text{total probability over the partition} \\ &= \E[X]. && \text{definition} \end{aligned}
∎
