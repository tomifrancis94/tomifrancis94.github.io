8 What the theorem means

Representation, not explanation. u was built out of the preferences. The theorem says the preferences can be described as expected-utility maximising. It does not say the agent computes anything, or that u is a quantity in her head that causes the preferences.

What rationality requires, on this view. Not “calculate expectations and choose the biggest”; rather, have preferences satisfying the axioms. Maximising expected utility is a consequence, not an instruction.

How many utility functions? Some u exists; the theorem picks out no particular one. Every a \cdot u + c with a > 0 does the same job, and nothing else does. Ratios of differences do mean something: they survive every rescaling.

8.1 Risk aversion, two senses#
1 / 3

Risk aversion about money is preferring \$50 for sure to a fair coin for \$100 or \$0. This is compatible with EUT: a concave u does it. Risk aversion about utility itself — preferring a sure thing to a gamble with the same expected utility — is ruled out.
