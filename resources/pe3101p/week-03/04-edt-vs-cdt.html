4.3 Why ain’cha rich?#

One-boxers walk away with a million. Two-boxers with a thousand. Over and over. Doesn’t that settle it?

Here’s a counterargument on the part of the CDT-ist. People who apply EDT are being rewarded by the demon before they take their decision. By CDT lights, they’re being rewarded for being irrational. But that doesn’t prove that they’re rational. However, it turns out that the situation is asymmetric: this can happen to the CDT-ist, but not to the EDT-ist. If being “irrational” is rewarded, EDT-ists will do the “irrational” thing associated with the reward.

4.4 The lesion#

A gene causes both a desire to smoke and cancer. Smoking itself causes nothing bad.

CancerNo cancer
Smoke-91
Don’t smoke-100
With \Cr(C \mid S) = 0.8 and \Cr(C \mid S^c) = 0.2:
\mathrm{EU}_e(S) = -9(0.8) + 1(0.2) = -7 , \qquad \mathrm{EU}_e(S^c) = -10(0.2) + 0(0.8) = -2 .

So the evidential theory says give up smoking.

4.5 Another causal diagram#

The gene causes both cancer and a desire to smoke; and the desire to smoke causes people to smoke; but smoking does not cause cancer. It makes no difference whether smoking causes cancer or merely indicates it. Either way, smoking is bad news.

4.6 The tickle defence#

The gene causes smoking by causing the desire. If you already know whether you have the desire, the desire is already the evidence. Acting on it tells you nothing further. So \Cr(C \mid S) = \Cr(C \mid S^c), and the case doesn’t arise.

U(A) = -1 - 98a , \qquad U(D) = -99 + 98a .
4.8 Unstable#

U(D) > U(A) whenever a > \nicefrac{1}{2}, and U(A) > U(D) whenever a < \nicefrac{1}{2}. Whichever act Tomi thinks he will perform, the other one looks better. The decision is unstable in the sense that making a decision to perform act A seems to make performing act A no longer rational. The paradox: it seems impossible to rationally decide and follow through. But it must be rational to decide and follow through on something!
